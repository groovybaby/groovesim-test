How to use / what the markers mean

In Mono mode the pink waveform is the sum of all enabled sources and determines the sideways path of the 3D groove. The draggable picture-in-picture end-on view is available in both Mono and Stereo modes. In Mono it moves only sideways at a constant groove depth; in Stereo a genuine two-channel audio file drives independent 45°/45° walls and the crosshair can move vertically as well. The 2D waveform, 3D groove scene, groove end-on cross-section and cartridge analysis are now four independent floating windows. Select or click any floating window to bring it in front of the others. Drag the 2D header to move its window, resize from either corner, or select Dock 2D to return it to the workspace. Dragging within the 2D plot still scrubs the recording. Drag the 3D pane header to move it (drag the 3D canvas itself to orbit), resize from either corner, or press Dock 3D to return it to the split layout. The other two windows remain independent. Drag either title bar to move its window; resize from either the top-left or bottom-right grip. The groove end-on window shows the stylus-position coordinates and the white-arrow horizontal/vertical velocity readings directly beneath the drawing, along with the arrow visibility control. The separate cartridge analysis window shows 45° vectors and electrical outputs at 33⅓ RPM. Drag left or right directly on the 2D waveform to scrub the recording at the current horizontal zoom, keeping the orange centre marker fixed; playback pauses while scrubbing. Use the 1/1,000× or 1/10,000× inspection speeds to watch individual oscillations; the lateral motion is (L+R)/2 and vertical motion is (L−R)/2. A left-only signal moves the groove diagonally one way, a right-only signal the other, and identical L+R gives pure lateral motion. The orange dot and optional rounded stylus indicate the same groove position. The optional stylus has a straight cylindrical stem and a taller conical nose with no oversized collar. The original red V groove reference remains visible. Its rounded contact tip has a nominal 15.24 µm radius and rides on the groove walls. All dimensions share the groove micrometre scale, but unmeasured assembly dimensions are illustrative. In the cartridge voltage box, both L and R voltages are active in Mono mode: they coincide because V = 0. The blue and amber waveforms show the same output in mono and separate waveforms in stereo. In the stereo stylus inset, the pink guide shows lateral displacement, the green guide shows vertical displacement, and the faint 22 ms history trail shows recent groove-bottom positions (not another groove); neither guide represents a separate groove. Each source has its own modulation in µm, which changes its 2D trace, its contribution to the 3D groove, and its audio loudness; 2D zoom can shrink or enlarge the traces in both directions without affecting the sound or groove. The fine surface grid on the vinyl stays fixed while the groove weaves across it. Drag the 3D view to rotate, scroll over it to zoom. In mono mode the groove stays 60 µm wide and 30 µm deep; in stereo, independent wall modulations can change the apparent groove width and depth, as a 45/45 cut does. The Movement magnification buttons in the end-on groove window exaggerate the visible groove modulation in both the end-on and 3D drawings (not the sound, actual displacement readings, groove dimensions or cartridge voltages). Use 1× for true displacement; 4× or 8× to see the ripples more clearly. A longer groove length shows more cycles. The end-on crosshair is the groove-bottom tracking point. Its optional white arrow starts at that crosshair and shows the current velocity direction, while the adjacent numerical readouts distinguish displacement (µm) from velocity (cm/s); velocity arrow length is scaled against a short surrounding interval for visibility, not a physical distance. Velocity, estimated voltages, vector arrows and voltage traces always reference 33⅓ RPM, regardless of the slowed inspection playback speed. This does not change stylus position, animation, audio or groove geometry. Show conical stylus toggles the semi-transparent cylinder, shoulder and cone. The rounded diamond tip contacts the 45° groove walls above the groove bottom. The original softer lighting is restored. The stereo groove walls use soft directional surface shading, without an artificial dark valley. Faint independent wall-relief lines are concentrated around sharper bends and omitted on unmodulated walls. Tick Peak shading to emphasise positive crests with denser, darker guides while leaving troughs smoother; untick to restore equal peak-and-trough curvature guides. Use the Line width and Line darkness sliders in the top-right of the 3D view to adjust the relief guides live without changing the groove geometry. They open at minimum width (0.3 px) and maximum darkness (100%). The end-on view also shows illustrative signed left and right cartridge voltages on their corresponding 45° wall axes. Voltages are proportional to groove-wall velocity at 33⅓ RPM, using a nominal 5 mV per 5 cm/s sensitivity; these are not direct measurements of a real cartridge. In stereo mode the blue left wall and amber right wall match the end-on inset; the pink 3D line follows the actual groove bottom; the vertical component is shown in the 2D waveform and end-on inset. Greensleeves has three options: plucked-string simulation, original electronic melody, and a real guitar recording you can download and load locally. The selected PCM waveform is shared by audio, the 2D trace and 3D groove. The real recording is converted to mono and normalised to the selected groove modulation in µm. The recording itself is not bundled: download the MP3 and select it using the file picker. Ode to Joy and tones remain synthesised. Use each source’s ON/OFF switch and modulation slider to hear and see it independently or in combination.
